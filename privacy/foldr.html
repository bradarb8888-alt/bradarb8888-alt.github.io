Foldr
Privacy Policy

Short version: Foldr is a file manager, and your files are your business. It has no ads, no analytics, no accounts and no tracking. It doesn't send your files or anything about you to me or anyone else. The only time it talks to a network is when you switch on one of its network features yourself.

Your files stay on your device

Foldr reads, moves, copies, compresses and deletes files on your phone or tablet because you asked it to. It keeps a few things in its own private storage so it works well: your settings, bookmarks, the search index, thumbnails, and the recycle bin. None of that leaves the device.

No ads, no analytics, no Google services

There's no advertising, analytics, crash reporting or tracking code in Foldr. It doesn't use Google Play Services or Firebase. You don't sign up or log in to anything.

All files access

On newer Android versions Foldr asks for "All files access". A file manager can't do much if it can only see some of your folders, so this permission is what lets it browse and manage your storage. It's only used for that. If you'd rather not grant it, Foldr runs in a limited mode where you pick folders yourself.

Network features (only when you turn them on)

Foldr has the Internet permission so these features can work. Each one stays off until you start it:

  • Wi-Fi transfer lets another device on your own local network (for example, a laptop on the same Wi-Fi) send files to or get files from your device through a browser. It only accepts connections from your local network, it needs a PIN shown on your screen, and it shuts itself off when you leave the screen or after 15 minutes of no activity.
  • SMB, FTP/FTPS, SFTP and WebDAV connect to servers you add yourself. Foldr talks directly to that server. The addresses and logins you save are kept on your device and aren't sent anywhere else.
  • Cloud storage (Dropbox, OneDrive, Google Drive) is switched off in version 1.0. If it's added later, this page will be updated first.

Root access

Root mode is optional and off by default. If your device is rooted and you turn it on, Foldr can reach system folders. It doesn't send anything anywhere because of it.

The vault

Files you put in the vault are encrypted on your device with a key held by Android's secure keystore, and unlocked with your fingerprint, face or screen lock. I can't open them, and there's no copy anywhere else. If you uninstall Foldr or lose the key, the vault can't be recovered.

Backups

Foldr opts out of Android cloud backup and device-to-device transfer. That covers the vault too, so its contents aren't copied to Google's backup service.

Notifications

Foldr may ask to show notifications so you can see the progress of long copies, moves or transfers. That's all they're used for.

Sharing

When you share a file from Foldr, Android hands it to the app you choose. What happens next is up to that app and its own privacy policy.

Deleting your data

Clearing Foldr's storage in Android Settings or uninstalling it removes its settings, index, thumbnails and vault. Your own files in normal folders stay where they are unless you delete them.

Children

Foldr is a general-audience tool and isn't aimed at children. It collects no personal information from anyone.
